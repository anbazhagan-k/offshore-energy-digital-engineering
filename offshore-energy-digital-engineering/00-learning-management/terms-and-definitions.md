# Terms and Definitions

**Purpose:** A cumulative glossary of technical, project, asset and Digital Engineering terminology encountered during this learning programme.

## How this glossary is maintained

- Add terms when the relevant lesson or study topic has been completed and reviewed.
- Include a concise definition, the context in which the term is used, and an example where useful.
- Group entries by subject and keep wording understandable to someone entering offshore energy.
- Add the source lesson so the definition can be revisited.
- Do not treat a term as mastered merely because it appears here; use the lesson exercises and review questions to test understanding.
- If a term is discussed as an early clarification before a lesson is complete, it may be recorded as **Introduced — review with lesson**.

## Excel and Data Analytics

| Term | Definition | Example / context | Learning status |
|---|---|---|---|
| IF function | A logical function that tests a condition and returns one value when the condition is TRUE and another when it is FALSE. | `=IF(B2>5,"High","Normal")` classifies an equipment quantity. | Practised — additional exercises pending |
| Logical test | A condition evaluated as TRUE or FALSE, often using a comparison operator. | `B2>=20` checks whether a pressure value is at least 20 bar. | Practised |
| Comparison operator | A symbol used to compare two values, such as equal to, greater than or less than. | `>=` means greater than or equal to; `<>` means not equal to. | Practised |
| Nested IF | An IF function placed inside another IF function's result argument to evaluate multiple possible conditions in sequence. | Classifying equipment pressure as Very Low, Low, Normal, High or Critical. | Practised — additional exercise pending |
| VALUE function | Converts a text string that represents a number into a numeric value. | `VALUE(B2)` can convert numeric text before a comparison in an IF formula. | Used in practice |
| Boundary value | A value exactly at a threshold where the classification may change. | If the rule is pressure greater than or equal to 20, exactly 20 must meet the condition. | Practised |

## Offshore Energy Fundamentals

| Term | Definition | Example / context | Learning status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acreage | A defined geographical area over which a company or licence holder has rights to explore for or develop oil and gas, subject to the applicable licence or contract. | An offshore exploration block covering 3,000 km². Acreage describes the area, not the quantity of oil or gas. | Introduced — review with Lesson 01 |
| Upstream | The part of the oil and gas value chain concerned with exploration, appraisal, field development and production. | Seismic surveys, exploration wells, subsea trees and offshore production platforms. | Introduced — review with Lesson 01 |
| Midstream | Activities and infrastructure that gather, process, transport and store hydrocarbons between production and downstream markets. | Gathering pipelines, gas processing, compressor stations, export terminals and storage. | Introduced — review with Lesson 01 |
| Downstream | Activities that refine or process hydrocarbons into products and distribute or sell those products to customers. | Oil refining, petrochemical production and fuel distribution. | Introduced — review with Lesson 01 |
| Oil and gas value chain | The connected activities through which hydrocarbons are explored for, produced, transported, processed and delivered to markets. It is commonly described using upstream, midstream and downstream segments. | A producing offshore field connected by pipeline to an LNG facility and then to gas customers. | Introduced — review with Lesson 01 |
